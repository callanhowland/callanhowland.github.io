Philosophy

In logic courses I introduce proofs through in-class fill-in-the-blank proofs that are left progressively emptier as the term goes on, with plenty of in-class practice and credit for good-faith attempts. I teach the semantics visually, leaning heavily on Venn diagrams and diagrammatic depictions of logical relations.

Course Development, Rutgers University

2026
CogSci 302: Computational Cognitive Modelling
New 300-level course, approved by the curriculum committee in 2026
Description
An introduction to what a model of human cognition is, why to build one, and how to evaluate it, across topics in decision, memory, perception, and language. No programming background is required: students run and modify supplied model code in R rather than writing models from scratch.
