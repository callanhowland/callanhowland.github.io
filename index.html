Callan Howland

I am a postdoctoral fellow at the Rutgers Center for Cognitive Science. I received my PhD in Philosophy from Rutgers University in 2024. I work in social and feminist philosophy, philosophy of language, and epistemology. A persistent question runs through my work: how do social dynamics shape decision and interation? I purse this question across language, cognition, and epistemology with a focus on gender, sexuality, disability, and collective political action. In language, I often study things like word choice and conversational role power dynamics (e.g. how it matters what name or pronoun a speaker uses, as well as the extent of speaker power to guide conversation). This work covers trans chosen names, and the conversational dynamics required to take them up, the linguistic mechanisms involved in misgendering, and when slurs and other expressive language becomes central to what a conversation is about. In epistemology, I look at how trust and deference impact the dynamics of group decision. I argue that deference to the marginalized can build political solidarity. I look at case studies including the AIDS crisis, and, in ongoing work, anti-Vietnam war action, and disability organizing that lead to the passage of the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA). In cognition In cognition, I run experiments on how social features shape people's representations of space and quantity, and on how people coordinate perspectives in conversation. Throughout, I use formal tools from logic, formal semantics and pragmatics, and Bayesian modelling (in the PRIME lab with Pernille Hemmer) to make these questions precise and testable.
